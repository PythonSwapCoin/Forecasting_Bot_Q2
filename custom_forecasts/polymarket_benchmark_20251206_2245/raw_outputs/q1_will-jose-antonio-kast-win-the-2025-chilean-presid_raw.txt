However, beginning with Sebastián Piñera's 2009 victory, Chilean politics entered what observers characterized as a pendulum pattern, with voters consistently rejecting incumbent coalitions regardless of ideological positioning[31]. This anti-incumbent tendency has proven remarkably consistent: in every presidential election cycle from 2009 onward, Chilean voters have systematically preferred the opposition to the governing party[17][30].

Understanding the 2005-2010 period proves crucial for contexualizing Kast's subsequent rise. Facing the 2005 presidential contest, the center-right Alianza coalition featured both Joaquín Lavín of the Independent Democratic Union (UDI) and Sebastián Piñera of National Renewal (RN), with the two right-wing parties unable to agree on a single candidate[26][29]. The coalition governments under Concertación rule from 1990 through 2010 consolidated Augusto Pinochet's neoliberal economic model while gradually introducing limited redistributive social programs, creating what scholars describe as an unstable equilibrium that satisfied neither business interests demanding unrestricted market freedom nor social movements pressing for genuine economic transformation[14][26].
